## Transferable editing rules

**P01 · Terror versus banal social complaint** — *When:* Genuinely ominous enemy or location. *Then:* Preserve atmospheric gameplay; reinterpret monster behavior as an everyday social failing (bad manners, poor work conditions, etc.). *Examples:* Dragon has personal-space issues; Knight needs benefits. *Avoid:* Humor must depend on observed behavior; no context-free one-liners.

**P02 · Confidence punished in under five seconds** — *When:* Player makes a taunt, confident prediction, or dismissive claim. *Then:* Keep unbroken boast → contradictory gameplay → short acknowledgement of error.. *Examples:* third-hit dare; dragon behind the opponent; ladder boast. *Avoid:* Do not fabricate gameplay timing; retain genuine outcomes.

**P03 · Fear expressed as bureaucratic politeness** — *When:* Player retreats or refuses a dangerous encounter. *Then:* Pair frantic movement with calm, formal verbal refusal or rationalization.. *Examples:* I respectfully decline; strategic retreat. *Avoid:* Use sparingly to preserve tension.

**P04 · Loot and expectation anticlimax** — *When:* Player risks death to obtain underwhelming loot. *Then:* Show cost and reward, then offer overenthusiastic or defeated commentary.. *Examples:* club after dragon burns; disappointing knight reward. *Avoid:* Ground joke in item value/usefulness where possible.

**P05 · Literalize spoken idiom visually** — *When:* Narrator makes a comparison with a concrete symbolic form. *Then:* On linguistic climax, insert brief graphic that illustrates the actual meaning.. *Examples:* dividing by zero on number of cares. *Avoid:* Never add mathematical or other effects at random.

**P06 · Recurring nemesis carries the B-story** — *When:* Same enemy or obstacle recurs. *Then:* Preserve first encounter, second failure/escalation, third successful or failed resolution; attach narrative memory.. *Examples:* mimic chest three rounds; knight pursue → defeat. *Avoid:* A callback must refer to a real prior event.

**P07 · Overreaching simile** — *When:* Threat or difficulty is unusually disproportionate. *Then:* Use one unexpected analogy then return quickly to unembellished action.. *Examples:* pencil versus impossible overthrow. *Avoid:* Avoid copying creator-specific similes; write originals.

**P08 · Meta editor collaboration** — *When:* Player knowingly addresses edit or future self. *Then:* Treat the spoken editing instruction as setup; time music/SFX to fulfill it.. *Examples:* future me cue battle music. *Avoid:* Never let editor music preempt the joke.

**P09 · Humanize AI and objects** — *When:* NPC behaves oddly or loot item name has a double meaning. *Then:* Give an enemy/object mundane personality, existential worries or occupational motives.. *Examples:* guard attacks boxes; Deep Battle Axe is misunderstood. *Avoid:* Tie the trait to an actual observation or name.

**P10 · Panic → mundane understatement** — *When:* Jump scare, death, close call. *Then:* Let reaction hit; end with remarkably ordinary or polite response.. *Examples:* mimic causes trust issues; fire definitely hurts. *Avoid:* Do not force sarcastic response into moments requiring quiet.

**P11 · Failure scoreboard without overcutting** — *When:* A meaningful actual death or failure. *Then:* Keep natural gameplay; let restrained tally update function as unsentimental narrative memory.. *Examples:* death counter 13→16. *Avoid:* Avoid treating every change in camera as an edited cut.

**P12 · Fearful antihero instead of power fantasy** — *When:* Confrontation where escape may be sensible. *Then:* Make avoidance itself into a protagonist preference, then let consequences contradict the plan.. *Examples:* try no battle; dragon trap backfires. *Avoid:* Keep agency and progression; do not make whole video only avoidance.

**P13 · Black-comedy philosophy** — *When:* World appears relentlessly miserable or hostile. *Then:* Make despair/existentialism a darkly ironic character trait of the setting, coupled with speaker self-awareness.. *Examples:* existential skeletons; life is meaningless and then you die. *Avoid:* Use situation-driven fictional dark humor, not automatic cruelty to real people.

**P14 · Premise extension (three-step riff)** — *When:* New noun or situation allows a word association. *Then:* Introduce the hook; expand its implications; end with absurd consequence or self-directed undercut.. *Examples:* deserter trousers fashion/fat pants; Deep Battle Axe as emo. *Avoid:* Do not spend multiple sentences riffing unless action allows it.
